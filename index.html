For developers

Stellacuity can also be driven from your own software. A simple command-line interface exposes the same deconvolution and noise reduction as the window, so imaging suites, scripts and automated pipelines can hand an image to Stellacuity and collect the result - no window, no clicks.

Stellacuity.exe sharpen INPUT OUTPUT [--preset light|normal|heavy] [--sharpening 1.5-8]
                [--halos 0-100] [--nonstellar 1.5-8] [--no-protect] [--cpu] [--depth 16|32]

Stellacuity.exe denoise INPUT OUTPUT [--preset light|normal|heavy] [--strength 0-100] [--cpu] [--depth 16|32]

Files in, files out

Mono or colour FITS, XISF, TIFF, JPEG or PNG in. Out, by the output's extension: 32-bit floating-point FITS or TIFF (add --depth 16 for 16-bit TIFF), JPEG or PNG.

Easy to follow

Plain-text progress on standard output - PROGRESS <0-1> <message> and NOTE <message>, ending with DONE <output> or ERROR <message> - with exit code 0 on success, so a progress bar takes a few lines.

Questions

Which images work best?

Linear (unstretched) stacks, before any stretching or other sharpening. For the best results, work in this order:

  1. Crop away the ragged stacking edges.
  2. Remove the gradient, leaving a small positive background rather than clipping it to zero.
  3. Colour calibrate colour images, keeping the data linear.
  4. Sharpen with Stellacuity.
  5. Reduce noise with Stellacuity.
  6. Stretch and carry on with your usual creative processing.

Already have a stretched image, such as a JPEG or a 16-bit TIFF? Reduce noise works well on those too. Sharpen is meant for linear data and may refuse a strongly stretched image, because stretching changes the shape of the stars it measures.

Do I need to enter my telescope, camera or pixel scale?

No. Stellacuity measures the star shape across the image from its own stars.

Does it work with colour (one-shot colour) images?

Yes, experimentally: each colour channel is sharpened and cleaned separately, so it takes about three times as long. Mono images are the main use for now.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs on your own computer, and your images are never uploaded. Stellacuity goes online to activate your licence key and, unless you switch it off, to check for a newer version at start-up.

How does the trial work?

The full program runs for 7 days from the first time you start it. You can sharpen, reduce noise and compare as much as you like, and save up to 3 finished results. After that it asks for a licence key; your settings and images are untouched.

Why "beta"?

Stellacuity works well but is still being improved - feedback is very welcome, and beta buyers get every update to deconvolution and noise reduction.

About

Stellacuity is made by John Hawkes, an amateur astrophotographer in the United Kingdom.

John studied theoretical physics at the University of Kent in the United Kingdom in the early 1980s, then took a Master of Science in software engineering, specialising in parallel computing. He spent the next twenty years as a software engineer, on work as diverse as computational fluid dynamics, futures-market modelling, operating systems for transputers and a wide range of business applications, before retraining for a career in medicine, where he became a lead MRI radiographer - a working life spent between physics, high-performance software and the art of getting clear images out of difficult data.

Under the night sky, he images from an observatory he built himself in his own garden, where his main instrument is a 12-inch Ritchey-Chrétien telescope and much of the equipment runs on firmware he has written himself. Every example on this page is his own data - the same kind of data you bring home from a night under the stars, with all its soft stars, seeing and corner aberrations.

Stellacuity is the result of a long effort to recover the sharpness that the atmosphere and optics take away, while staying true to the data. It is developed and supported by John himself, so questions, suggestions and examples of your own results are always welcome at support@stellacuity.com.
